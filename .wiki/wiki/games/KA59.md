## Mechanic reconciliation (validated 2026-07-15, task #49)

The old "two agents push blocks into goal cells" (classic Sokoban) framing is
INACCURATE. Reading the game source + a live probe shows:
- ACTION1-4 directly move the ONE active ring PIECE by one cell (blocked by
  walls); ACTION6 clicks to switch which piece is active. WIN = every hollow
  FRAME target is filled by a piece exactly centred inside it — a PLACEMENT
  condition, not boxes-to-goals.
- The "push" is a MOMENTUM-SLIDE LAUNCH (`explode-*` projectile in the source):
  moving the active piece INTO another launches a multi-tick slide that shoves
  pieces along the lane — used to cross an otherwise-impassable wall (measured
  col 27→42), NOT a 1-cell Sokoban push.

Consequence for kernels: the new generic `plan_push`
(`src/admorphiq/kernels/paths.py`) models CLASSIC 1-cell Sokoban push
reachability (route a box to a target, repositioning the pusher to the
opposite side each push) — a reusable primitive for TRUE box-to-goal Sokoban
games, validated on synthetic scenarios (`tests/test_kernels_paths.py`). It
does NOT match ka59's ring-placement win or its momentum-slide push, so ka59
was deliberately NOT rewired to it (that would mis-model the game and risk
regressing the careful existing adapter). ka59's real lever is joint-planning
coordination + a momentum-slide-specific successor model — a distinct
follow-up, banked honestly here.

## L0 clearing recipe VALIDATED live (2026-07-15, task follow-up) — reopen, not banked-as-unclear

A joint-placement pass PROVED L0 is clearable (reproduced `levels_completed → 1`
live) — turning the vague "joint planning gap" into a demonstrated recipe with
the exact composition:
- **Detect**: 2 ring PIECES (small class) at (30,18),(30,27); 2 FRAME targets
  with inner top-lefts (33,12) and (27,45). Fill = piece outer-bbox top-left ==
  frame inner top-left. Pieces move a FIXED 3px step (ACTION1=up3, 2=down3,
  3=left3, 4=right3); only the ACTIVE piece moves; ACTION6 clicks a piece's
  top-left to select it (measured reliable).
- **The wall**: the frame at (27,45) is across an impassable band (~col 30-33).
  A piece walking right stalls at (30,30) — it needs a LAUNCH.
- **The launch (measured)**: colliding the active piece into another piece
  shoves the STRUCK piece a fixed momentum distance along the lane — piece
  (30,27) launched to (30,42), crossing the wall (a multi-tick slide; both
  rings go undetectable mid-animation, positions then re-read).
- **Winning sequence (measured live)**: (1) collide piece1 into piece2 → piece2
  at (30,42); (2) select the far piece, walk it up+right to (27,45) → frame
  filled; (3) select the near piece, walk it (left-then-down; down is blocked
  from (30,27), so route left to col 12 first) to (33,12) → both filled → WIN.

Assignment (`assign_pairs` by Manhattan) pairs (30,18)→(33,12) and
(30,27)→(27,45).

## L0 CLEARED — orchestrator launch fired (2026-07-15, R56, 1/7 measured)

The joint-plan orchestrator (`_orchestrate`/`_orch_place`/`_orch_launch` in
`src/admorphiq/adapters25/ka59.py`) now clears L0 live: `levels_completed → 1`,
`game_score = 0.0205` (`scripts/rounds/script25_ka59_orch3`). The single bug
that had held it at 0/7 was a **collision-misread poisoning the launch
corridor**:

- The place phase walks the far piece to the wall edge (30,27→30,30), then
  flags a launch. The pusher must reach the cell one step behind the (now
  wall-adjacent) launchee and drive INTO it.
- Every approach step toward the launchee reads as "did not move" — the
  engine consumes it as the momentum push, not a 1-cell advance — so
  `_record_blocked` banked each approach cell as a PERMANENT wall. The
  optimistic router then beelined AWAY from the launchee forever; the launch
  was unreachable.

Two measured facts fixed it: (1) the slide fires ONLY when the pusher moves
into the launchee's EXACT cell (`active + unit == launchee`) — a hit from two
cells away leaves the launchee unmoved (launch counter ticked, nothing slid);
(2) `_orch_launch` un-poisons the collinear-behind approach corridor
(`_LAUNCH_CORRIDOR_DEPTH`) from `_known_blocked` before routing, since those
are approach cells the launchee itself traversed, not confirmed walls. The
pusher then reaches `behind` cleanly and drives in; the launchee slides across
the wall (measured 30,30→30,45), both frames fill, L0 wins.

**Still open (L1+)**: banked as a GENUINELY NEW structure — see the L1
characterization below. The legacy per-call `_decide` remains the fallback for
anything the 2-piece orchestrator doesn't model, so the 1/7 floor cannot
regress. No hardcoded per-level coordinates: piece/target cells, assignment,
launch axis, and step delta are all derived per level from `closed_frames` +
`size_clusters` + `assign_pairs` + measured `dir_map`.

## L1 launch-coordination round — BANKED (2026-07-16, dedicated round, ka59 closes for the sprint)

**Outcome: 1/7 held (floor deterministic ×2 before + after, ka59-38d34dbb @600,
0.0205; no adapter change). The launch table could not be measured empirically
and the coordination is a multi-round subsystem, not a bounded round — banked
per the stop rule with the measured facts + first-infeasibility evidence below.**

The plan was: build the launch table via the live oracle (drive a launch, read
the exit cell), then a `configuration_path` joint search with launch edges. Both
halves hit concrete, measured walls:

1. **No launch ever fires with the current machinery (measured).** A full
   600-action real adapter run on L1 tracked the min column of every color-14
   region: it stayed at **col 30 the entire run — zero entries into the band
   (cols 21-29) or the left chamber (col < 21)**. Pieces reach the band edge and
   stop; nothing crosses. So there is no existing crossing to read a table from.
2. **The launch table is not cleanly measurable on L1 in a raw harness — a
   structural reason, not tooling laziness:**
   - L1 pieces are **SOLID** (areas 8/16/32 cells), not single-hole rings, so
     `_piece_marker_color` returns None for them — the adapter's L0 active-piece
     marker mechanism does NOT apply; on L1 the active piece is identified by
     **movement probes only** (which themselves perturb the board).
   - Same-color solid pieces **MERGE** under `find_regions` when adjacent — and
     adjacency is exactly the launch setup (pusher one cell behind the
     launchee). Once merged they are one color-14 region, so position-tracking
     and the precise "pusher moves into the launchee's exact cell" collision
     (the L0 firing condition) cannot be observed or verified. Four harness
     iterations (color-14-cell selection fixed the earlier center-click bug; the
     merge defeated every attempt to read a launchee's post-shove exit).
3. **The coordination is worse than one-launch-per-piece.** Frame `(9,9)` is in
   the TOP-left chamber; a right-chamber piece must launch LEFT across the
   vertical band (cols 21-29) into the bottom-left chamber, then launch UP across
   the horizontal band (rows 24-29) into the top-left chamber — a **two-launch
   chain**, on top of not stranding the other three pieces. This is the
   "coordination search is non-trivial" trigger.

**Validated this round (the base a future subsystem builds on):** parse is
sound (BFS-reachability over `passable=not-color-2` found piece→frame paths for
all four; the hetero path fills 2/4 same-chamber); color-15 is confirmed
non-walkable (a selected area-32 piece stalls 9 steps at the boundary, never
entering color-15); size-match is rank-of-sorted-sizes (piece bbox area ==
frame inner area, off by the +2 border ring for a naive cell-count match).

**What a future (multi-round) launch subsystem needs**, in order: (a) a
solid-piece active-ID that survives merge — likely a movement-fingerprint or a
transient displacement probe, since there is no marker; (b) a launch-fire
primitive extending `_orch_launch`'s precise pusher-into-launchee collision to
solid extended pieces at a band edge, verified to actually cross; (c) THEN the
launch table via the live oracle (now that a fire primitive exists); (d) a
joint `configuration_path` over walk + launch edges including the two-launch
chain for `(9,9)`. Each is a round; this is the m0r0-L6-class effort, not a
bounded pass. ka59 stays **1/7** and closes for the sprint.

L1 is a **heterogeneous multi-size size-matched placement** puzzle, validated
both from the gold trace and live (script25, `most_common_color`=1):

- **4 target frames** (color-4 hollow rings), inner-bbox areas **9, 18, 18, 36**
  at inner-tls (51,51), (9,9), (39,54), (42,6).
- **4 pieces** (color-14 SOLID shapes via `find_regions`), bbox areas **9, 18,
  18, 36** at (54,36), (33,39), (42,33), (45,42). Match is **piece bbox area ==
  frame inner area** (piece color-14 CELL count is bbox−2, the center marker
  cell(s) being color-5/0). Rank-matching sorted sizes {8,16,16,32 cells} →
  {9,18,18,36 inner} pairs each piece to its frame; a naive exact-area match
  fails (off by the +2 border) — noted so the next round matches by rank, not
  equality.

**Parse validated live (2026-07-16)** — a fresh BFS-reachability prototype over
`passable = not-color-2` found valid paths piece→frame for all four
assignments (lengths 6–13), and the gated hetero path fills **2 of 4 frames**
(`(51,51)`, `(39,54)`), confirming parse + same-chamber routing are sound.

**CORRECTION — color-15 is a NON-WALKABLE BARRIER, crossing is a LAUNCH not a
walk (live-validated 2026-07-16, supersedes the "invisible walk" bank).** A
clean single-piece drive test: select the area-32 piece and step LEFT toward
the band. It advanced (45,42)→(45,39)→…→**(45,30) and then STALLED for 9
consecutive steps**, with color-15 immediately to its left the whole time — it
never entered a color-15 cell, visibly or invisibly. Pieces **cannot walk into
color-15**; they stop dead at the chamber/band boundary. The BFS prototype that
treated color-15 as passable therefore also only filled the two same-chamber
frames — the cross-band pieces physically cannot be walked across.

Reconciling with the gold trace (which showed pieces vanish in the band and
reappear on the far side with "no >6px jump between detected frames"): that is
the signature of a **momentum-slide LAUNCH**, identical to L0 — the piece is
undetectable during the multi-tick slide animation, so consecutive *detected*
frames never expose the jump. The earlier round mis-attributed the invisibility
to walking. `_passable_terrain` (color-15 treated passable) is built on the
wrong premise; it is inert for the floor (2/4 unchanged either way, since
walking can't cross regardless) but should be revisited when the crossing is
rebuilt as a launch.

**Remaining subsystem (banked — beyond one bounded pass)**: the two cross-band
frames `(9,9)` (top-left, across the full-width color-15 rows 24-29) and
`(42,6)` (bottom-left, across the vertical color-15 cols 21-29) require a
**4-piece launch coordination**: walk a cross-band piece to the band edge,
shove it across with a pusher piece (L0's momentum mechanic), and land it in
the far chamber, then walk to the frame — while not stranding the other three
pieces or blocking the same-chamber placements. Unlike the gold-only situation
the prior bank lamented, the **live engine is now a usable oracle** for the
launch exit cell (drive a launch, read where the piece reappears) — so the
exit model IS validatable; it simply needs its own round (launch-targeting,
band-exit measurement, multi-piece ordering). Same-chamber placement + the
validated parse are the base that round builds on.
