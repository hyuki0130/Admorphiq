## Mechanic reconciliation (validated 2026-07-15, task #49)

The old "two agents push blocks into goal cells" (classic Sokoban) framing is
INACCURATE. Reading the game source + a live probe shows:
- ACTION1-4 directly move the ONE active ring PIECE by one cell (blocked by
  walls); ACTION6 clicks to switch which piece is active. WIN = every hollow
  FRAME target is filled by a piece exactly centred inside it — a PLACEMENT
  condition, not boxes-to-goals.
- The "push" is a MOMENTUM-SLIDE LAUNCH (`explode-*` projectile in the source):
  moving the active piece INTO another launches a multi-tick slide that shoves
  pieces along the lane — used to cross an otherwise-impassable wall (measured
  col 27→42), NOT a 1-cell Sokoban push.

Consequence for kernels: the new generic `plan_push`
(`src/admorphiq/kernels/paths.py`) models CLASSIC 1-cell Sokoban push
reachability (route a box to a target, repositioning the pusher to the
opposite side each push) — a reusable primitive for TRUE box-to-goal Sokoban
games, validated on synthetic scenarios (`tests/test_kernels_paths.py`). It
does NOT match ka59's ring-placement win or its momentum-slide push, so ka59
was deliberately NOT rewired to it (that would mis-model the game and risk
regressing the careful existing adapter). ka59's real lever is joint-planning
coordination + a momentum-slide-specific successor model — a distinct
follow-up, banked honestly here.

## L0 clearing recipe VALIDATED live (2026-07-15, task follow-up) — reopen, not banked-as-unclear

A joint-placement pass PROVED L0 is clearable (reproduced `levels_completed → 1`
live) — turning the vague "joint planning gap" into a demonstrated recipe with
the exact composition:
- **Detect**: 2 ring PIECES (small class) at (30,18),(30,27); 2 FRAME targets
  with inner top-lefts (33,12) and (27,45). Fill = piece outer-bbox top-left ==
  frame inner top-left. Pieces move a FIXED 3px step (ACTION1=up3, 2=down3,
  3=left3, 4=right3); only the ACTIVE piece moves; ACTION6 clicks a piece's
  top-left to select it (measured reliable).
- **The wall**: the frame at (27,45) is across an impassable band (~col 30-33).
  A piece walking right stalls at (30,30) — it needs a LAUNCH.
- **The launch (measured)**: colliding the active piece into another piece
  shoves the STRUCK piece a fixed momentum distance along the lane — piece
  (30,27) launched to (30,42), crossing the wall (a multi-tick slide; both
  rings go undetectable mid-animation, positions then re-read).
- **Winning sequence (measured live)**: (1) collide piece1 into piece2 → piece2
  at (30,42); (2) select the far piece, walk it up+right to (27,45) → frame
  filled; (3) select the near piece, walk it (left-then-down; down is blocked
  from (30,27), so route left to col 12 first) to (33,12) → both filled → WIN.

Assignment (`assign_pairs` by Manhattan) pairs (30,18)→(33,12) and
(30,27)→(27,45).

## L0 CLEARED — orchestrator launch fired (2026-07-15, R56, 1/7 measured)

The joint-plan orchestrator (`_orchestrate`/`_orch_place`/`_orch_launch` in
`src/admorphiq/adapters25/ka59.py`) now clears L0 live: `levels_completed → 1`,
`game_score = 0.0205` (`scripts/rounds/script25_ka59_orch3`). The single bug
that had held it at 0/7 was a **collision-misread poisoning the launch
corridor**:

- The place phase walks the far piece to the wall edge (30,27→30,30), then
  flags a launch. The pusher must reach the cell one step behind the (now
  wall-adjacent) launchee and drive INTO it.
- Every approach step toward the launchee reads as "did not move" — the
  engine consumes it as the momentum push, not a 1-cell advance — so
  `_record_blocked` banked each approach cell as a PERMANENT wall. The
  optimistic router then beelined AWAY from the launchee forever; the launch
  was unreachable.

Two measured facts fixed it: (1) the slide fires ONLY when the pusher moves
into the launchee's EXACT cell (`active + unit == launchee`) — a hit from two
cells away leaves the launchee unmoved (launch counter ticked, nothing slid);
(2) `_orch_launch` un-poisons the collinear-behind approach corridor
(`_LAUNCH_CORRIDOR_DEPTH`) from `_known_blocked` before routing, since those
are approach cells the launchee itself traversed, not confirmed walls. The
pusher then reaches `behind` cleanly and drives in; the launchee slides across
the wall (measured 30,30→30,45), both frames fill, L0 wins.

**Still open (L1+)**: banked as a GENUINELY NEW structure — see the L1
characterization below. The legacy per-call `_decide` remains the fallback for
anything the 2-piece orchestrator doesn't model, so the 1/7 floor cannot
regress. No hardcoded per-level coordinates: piece/target cells, assignment,
launch axis, and step delta are all derived per level from `closed_frames` +
`size_clusters` + `assign_pairs` + measured `dir_map`.

## L1 — gated size-matched placement IMPLEMENTED; blocked on inter-chamber transport (2026-07-15, R56)

**Status: L1 does NOT clear (still 1/7); the size-matched placement path is
built + gated + tested and measurably fills 2 of 4 frames; the remaining
blocker is an inter-chamber transport mechanic, banked below.**

L1 is NOT a launch/momentum level and NOT an axis/budget variant of L0 — it is
a **heterogeneous multi-size size-matched placement** puzzle. Characterized
both from the gold trace (`data/traces/ka59.npz` idx13) AND live (script25
hash `9f096b4a`, `most_common_color`=1) — the two agree exactly:

- **4 target frames** (color-4 hollow rings) with inner-bbox areas **9, 18, 18,
  36** at inner-tls (51,51), (9,9), (39,54), (42,6).
- **4 pieces** (color-14 SOLID shapes, found by `find_regions`, NOT hollow
  rings) with bbox areas **9, 18, 18, 36** at (54,36), (33,39), (42,33),
  (45,42). Each piece matches exactly one frame BY SIZE.
- Gold (`is_gold` rows for level 1) places all four by **pure select+walk**
  sequences — zero collisions, zero momentum slides. My earlier "L1 uses a
  vertical launch (unit (3,0))" note was WRONG: that (3,0) was an artifact of
  the orchestrator mis-routing L1's under-detection, not a real launch.

**The gated placement path (implemented, measured working for its scope)**:

- Gate = frame inner-size-class count (`_frame_inner_areas`): >1 class → the
  heterogeneous path (`_classify_hetero` detects solid pieces via
  `find_regions`, size-matched `_assign`, marker-learning suppressed so all
  identification is movement-based); a single class → the untouched L0 ring
  model. **L0 stays byte-identical** (single class), verified by a determinism
  ×2 run staying at 1/7 @ 0.0205. Unit tests in `tests/test_adapters25_ka59.py`
  pin the gate, the size match, and the L0-invariant.
- MEASURED: the path correctly detects all 4 solid pieces + 4 frames, matches
  by size, and **fills 2 of the 4 frames** — exactly `(51,51)` and `(39,54)`.

**Why only 2 of 4 — the real remaining blocker (inter-chamber transport)**:
the board is split by color-15/color-2 barriers into THREE chambers. All 4
pieces START in the right chamber, which also contains frames `(51,51)` and
`(39,54)` (both fillable, and filled). Frames `(9,9)` (top-left chamber) and
`(42,6)` (bottom-left chamber) are UNREACHABLE by 3px-step walking — a
bg-connectivity flood (±3 moves) from the piece start reaches only the two
same-chamber frames. Crossing chambers needs a transport mechanic (the color-2
strips look like portals; or a launch-through-wall as on L0). Even at 3000
actions L1 stays at 2 frames filled, confirming it is structural, not budget.

**Distinct follow-up (NOT attempted here, per the stop rule)**: add an
inter-chamber transport layer (verify color-2 = portal vs a launch-through-wall
crossing; route a piece onto the transport, handle the post-transport
re-identification, then resume size-matched placement in the destination
chamber). The size-matched placement built this round is the foundation that
follow-up builds on.
