# LF52 — Peg Solitaire (jump-and-capture)

> Select a piece, jump it over an adjacent piece into an empty board slot; the
> jumped piece is CAPTURED and removed. Reduce the board to ONE piece to win
> (levels 6/7 win at two). First MIS-characterised in R56/R56b as an
> "unclearable position-independent animation"; that park was FALSIFIED in
> R56c by reading the source + driving the real env loop.

## Mechanics (CORRECTED — R56c, 2026-07-16)

**LF52 is PEG SOLITAIRE.** Source ground truth (`dghsidbuet` click dispatch):

- **ACTION6 hit-tests the clicked cell** (`rozmjeilxc`, a camera transform):
  - Clicking a **piece** (`fozwvlovdui`) SELECTS it — `xpcuvjyrgu` attaches a
    direction arrow (`lgbyiaitpdi`) for each LEGAL jump.
  - Clicking a legal **landing cell** fires the jump (`cfilhtifcb`): the piece
    hops two cells over an adjacent piece; the jumped piece is captured
    (`faretcdgmc`) and removed.
  - `vjafsffahp = [(0,-1),(1,0),(0,1),(-1,0)]` are the four jump directions.
- **Legality** (`qikmikecdf`): `P -> P+2d` is legal iff `P+d` holds a piece
  (`pymqmlkgzs`) AND `P+2d` is an empty board slot / pin (`posalhhmjq`, the
  `hupkpseyuim` cells).
- **WIN** fires when the board is reduced to ONE piece
  (`ddaguepwkt == 1` in `cfilhtifcb` → `tdcblgbfxw` → `win()`); levels 6/7 win
  at two. The per-level ACTION-COUNT budget (`asqvqzpfdi` vs 64 / 64*5 / 64*10)
  is the LOSE gate.
- ACTION1-4 shift `hupkpseyuim2` walls on some deep levels; **peg solitaire
  needs ONLY ACTION6 clicks** — L0 clears with zero cursor moves.

**Board is frame-parseable**: on a 6-pixel lattice, PIECES are one colour
(14 on L0, size ~12) and board SLOTS another colour (1 on L0, size ~16). A slot
with no piece on it is an empty hole a jump can land in. A lone colour-9
size-23 blob is the SELECTION/animation marker, NOT a piece — it must be
excluded (it was the bug that made the parse see a spurious 6th piece).

## Why the R56/R56b park was WRONG

R56b ran a validate-first probe that clicked arbitrary cells and saw the SAME
colour-9 growth at a fixed spot regardless of click position, and concluded the
click was an input-position-INDEPENDENT side-effect with no operator to plan
over. **That growth is the level-1 TUTORIAL HINT** (`sgxkqallyv` →
`bhdfjlqapap` blink), which fires on an EMPTY-space click and always points at
the same game-determined spot. The probe measured the hint, not the game
response — the classic "a probe only measures what its action exercises"
failure ([[../lessons/probe_validity_20260715]]). Clicking an actual PIECE (not
empty space) selects it and enables the jump. Live proof: 8 ACTION6 clicks (4
jumps) on L0's `(1,2)(2,2)(4,2)(5,3)(5,5)` line advanced `levels_completed`
0 → 1.

## Solution Pattern (frame-only, R56c — clears L0)

Faithful offline simulator + DFS (the sb26/sk48 method,
[[../lessons/faithful_offline_simulator_20260715]]):

1. Parse the visible frame into lattice sets: pieces (rarer cell colour) and
   cells (all board slots) via [[../concepts/sprite_cluster]] /
   `find_regions` on the 6px lattice.
2. DFS the jump graph offline for a sequence reducing to ONE piece (each jump
   removes the jumped piece); no live probing needed — the board is
   deterministic and fully decodable.
3. Replay: two clicks per jump — the piece centroid (select), then the landing
   centroid (jump). Clicks use detected pixel centroids, so the per-level
   camera offset does not matter for replay.

Adapter: `src/admorphiq/adapters25/lf52.py` (`_parse_board`, `_solve`).
Tests: `tests/test_adapters25_lf52.py` pin the simulator + parse.

## Scope / Limits — L1 blocker CORRECTED (R56d, 2026-07-16)

The reduce-to-one model clears L0 (full board on-screen, monochrome). L1 was
initially banked as "needs viewport reconstruction"; a bounded reconstruction
round MEASURED that framing to be WRONG. **L1 is not a viewport problem — it
needs a level-specific reveal-transport mechanic that the reduce-to-one DFS
cannot express, on ANY reconstruction of the board.**

- **The full static L1 board still has NO reduce-to-one solution.** From the
  source grid `grid2` (dev-time ground truth), L1's five pegs are `(1,1)(2,1)
  (4,1)(6,1)` in a TOP block (rows 0-2, cols 0-6) and `(6,7)` in a BOTTOM
  block (rows 6-7). The two blocks share ZERO board slots between rows 2 and
  6 — they are topologically DISCONNECTED for peg jumps. Running `_solve` on
  the fully reconstructed grid2 slot set returns `[]` (measured). So even a
  perfect viewport stitch does not unlock L1 — reconstruction is a red herring
  here.
- **The connector is a `kraubslpehi` conveyor/arrow track + a reveal mode, not
  a camera pan.** grid2 has an arrow path (`->`, `|`, `<--,---3`, `L----`)
  linking the two regions. Live probe (faithful passive read): jumps reduce
  the top block (5→4→3), and LANDING a peg on a per-level TARGET cell
  (`(0,1)/(2,1)/(4,1)` for L1) fires `pchvqimdvj` → `cncmupctrp`, which sets
  `zvcnglshzcx=True` (reveal mode) and spawns a bottom-left `cwyrzsciwms`
  button. A bottom-left-corner ACTION6 in reveal mode runs the scanline
  animation (`foztuzaztfm`) then `jhgacfwhoa` — which in the probe REDREW the
  board back to five pegs rather than connecting the regions. So the transport
  semantics are a staged reveal, only partially decoded, and did not compose a
  cross-region reduction within the pass.
- **Also on deep levels**: coloured red/blue pegs (`fozwvlovdui_red/_blue`)
  with pairing constraints (levels ≥ 6-8) and the two-piece win (levels 6/7).
- **Reopen pointer (corrected, harder than "reconstruct viewports")**: decode
  the `kraubslpehi` conveyor + reveal-mode transport (how reaching a target +
  the corner-click scanline actually relocates a peg across the arrow track),
  then model it as an operator ALONGSIDE the jump so search can cross regions.
  ⛔ Do NOT re-attempt "accumulate viewports → DFS on the full board" — it is
  MEASURED insufficient (static grid2 DFS = []); the wall is the transport
  mechanic, not the viewport window.
