> coupled GF(2) neighbourhood stencil (that reading was falsified by
> gold-trace decode). R56's `adapters25/ft09.py` reads the win condition

> or-better efficiency; a 5th level's mechanic is now SOLVED (Codex) but
> not yet integrated/re-smoked, and a 6th is fully decoded but unreached
> live (see Current Status).

## Observations

- The board is one or more 8-cell "rings" (a 3x3 button layout minus its
  own center) wrapped around a small glyph occupying the ring's center
  gap.
- Some rings are TRUNCATED by the frame edge — fewer than 8 real button
  members, the rest simply off-board or background (measured: both real
  truncated rings found so far had exactly 4 real members).
- Several levels are two-phase: the level-start board is a "decoy" where
  every discoverable ring already satisfies its own glyph (nothing to
  click); ONE click anywhere wholesale-replaces the entire visible region
  layout with a different, previously-invisible board — the level's real
  puzzle.
- Clicking a cell always advances it one step through a colour cycle that
  is discovered empirically per board (2-value on levels 1-3/5, a 3-value
  cycle `[9,8,12]` on level 4).
- On one measured board (level 6), a single click can advance TWO cells
  simultaneously: the clicked cell, and one other cell at a fixed,
  geometrically-measured offset (8 rows outward from the ring's own
  center, same column). What looks like a two-cell "swap" is actually two
  cells independently stepping the SAME cycle from different starting
  phases.
- On level 5, 3 of the glyph-gap positions are themselves STATEFUL
  buttons, not pure target-prediction glyphs: clicking one toggles its
  OWN colour 14<->15 AND every existing position painted with the
  board's "action stencil" ink (measured: 6) around it — a real cross-
  toggle side effect, not a separate "commit" invariant. These control
  buttons are ALSO ordinary target cells in their own right, constrained
  by other glyphs exactly like any field cell. See Mechanics Hypothesis.
- Ring-member-count filtering can silently DROP real glyphs: two of
  level 5's glyphs only have 3 real neighbours (below the 4-member floor
  the adapter's discovery uses to reject noise), and both turned out to
  be load-bearing — they constrain the 3 control buttons above.

## Mechanics Hypothesis

**Current model (R56, gold-trace verified on 4 of 6 levels; live-cleared
on those same 4). Read this as a JOURNEY, not a single derivation — every
earlier hypothesis below was falsified by a specific, measured
observation, and the falsification is what motivated the next one.**

### The confirmed constraint-satisfaction rule

For every board cell, collect a constraint from EVERY discovered glyph
whose 8-neighbour reach includes it — not just the nearest glyph (see
"The coverage-scoping near-miss" below for why "nearest" is wrong). Ink
colour **0** at a compass position means that cell's colour must **equal**
the covering glyph's own center (marker) colour; ink colour **2** means it
must **differ** from the marker; ink colour **3** means no constraint at
all (a truncated ring's missing compass position, or simply background at
a lattice point). ALL of a cell's covering constraints must hold
simultaneously. A cell needing more than one click walks the board's own
MEASURED colour cycle one step per click (never a hardcoded palette).

This rule is verified BYTE-FOR-BYTE against gold traces on levels 1-4 and
6 (0-indexed 0-3 and 5): every covered cell's colour at the true gold win
moment satisfies its full constraint set, zero exceptions, on every one of
those boards.

### The falsification journey (each step is a real, measured event)

1. **Original hypothesis (pre-R56, historical `lights_out` solver)**: a
   coupled GF(2) neighbourhood-toggle system — clicking a cell flips a
   SET of cells (possibly a stencil `A[i][j]`), solved via linear algebra.
   Cleared L1 live but stalled L2+ for years; see
   [[../lessons/gf2_lights_out_stencil_20260423]]. **Falsified**: gold-
   trace replay showed a click only EVER changes the clicked cell — there
   is no coupling to measure on levels 1-4. (Levels 5-6 reopened a
   qualified version of "coupling" much later — see below — but not the
   ORIGINAL neighbourhood-stencil shape; level 6's coupling is a fixed,
   measured second-cell effect discovered empirically per board, not a
   general stencil solved from scratch every level.)
2. **Odd-one-out hypothesis** (an early guess: the target is "whichever
   cell doesn't match its neighbours"): **falsified** directly — the true
   base state of the first captured ring is uniformly one colour, no
   outlier exists to match against.
3. **Settle-frame / render-lag hypothesis** (an early guess that an
   anomalous reading was a WA30/RE86-class "read too early" bug): checked
   directly via `next_frames[N-1] == frames[N]` byte-equality across a
   level transition — **falsified**, the frame WAS the genuine post-
   transition board, no missing settle step.
4. **The glyph-decode rule itself** (Section above) was then derived
   directly from gold click-effect tables, and verified exactly on level 1
   (byte-for-byte predicted-vs-gold click set on a 4-ring board where 3
   rings were pre-solved and only the 4th's exact gold clicks matched the
   decode's prediction).
5. **Decoy -> reveal two-phase boards**: discovered on levels 2 and 6 (a
   third apparent case, level 4, was LATER shown by item 11 to be a
   different phenomenon entirely, not a real decoy) — a board that reads
   as "zero mismatches, nothing to click" is not necessarily solved; it
   may be a decoy whose first click reveals a DIFFERENT board (region
   layout wholesale-replaced, not merely recoloured). The adapter treats
   "nothing unsatisfied" as a trigger-click signal, not a giveup.
6. **The L3-formula redundant-click claim, and the coverage-scoping near-
   miss**: a Codex-derived 3-colour-cycle formula for level 4 (0-indexed
   3) claimed one gold click was redundant (both of two candidate colours
   satisfied that cell's constraints). A LIVE deterministic replay
   OMITTING that click **failed to clear the level** — direct evidence
   against the redundant-click claim. Root-caused by reading the
   (obfuscated) environment source's own completion check
   (`environment_files/ft09/*/ft09.py`, `cgj()`, read AFTER deriving from
   data, verification only): the "redundant" cell was actually covered by
   a THIRD glyph the original derivation had missed — its constraint set
   was `{≠12, ≠12, ≠9}`, uniquely satisfied only by colour 8, not
   "either 9 or 8" as claimed. **Fix**: coverage must be built by checking
   EVERY discovered glyph's full 8-neighbour reach against every cell, not
   scoping to "nearby" glyphs — cells near where rings meet can be, and
   measurably are, covered by 2 or 3 glyphs simultaneously. Re-derived
   with full enumeration: 18/18 cells match gold's win state exactly, and
   the previously-"redundant" cell turns out to be uniquely determined,
   not ambiguous. This is the single most load-bearing lesson from this
   round — see [[../lessons/ft09_glyph_decode_20260715]] for the full
   writeup.
7. **Truncated rings** (level 6): a ring can have FEWER than 8 real
   button members — 4-5 measured — with the missing compass positions'
   ink reading 3. Discovery was relaxed to accept these once a minimum-
   member floor (4, the measured floor) rules out spurious 1-2-member
   noise on multi-ring boards.
8. **Cell coupling** (level 6): a single click sometimes advances a
   SECOND cell too, at a fixed geometric offset (measured: 8 rows
   outward, same column). What looks like a "swap" (two cells trading
   colours) is really two independent cycle-steps from different starting
   phases — verified against ALL 13 real gold clicks on that level with
   zero exceptions, including correctly predicting "no second cell
   changes" whenever the offset position is background.
9. **An apparent third glyph type — "commit" glyphs** (level 5, INITIAL
   reading, since superseded by item 11): 3 of the 9 candidate glyph
   positions on this board were initially read as harmless false-positive
   noise (their ink values, 6 and 14, don't match the confirmed 0/2/3
   vocabulary, so they contribute zero constraints under the confirmed
   rule). Deeper investigation found they are NOT noise: clicking directly
   on one of these glyphs' own gap positions (not a field cell) produces a
   real, structured effect on that glyph's 4 edge (N/W/E/S) members. At
   this point in the investigation the effect looked like a "commit"
   invariant ("normalize toward at most one marked"), and TWO live
   negative replays (clicking exactly the confirmed-rule-predicted 9
   target cells; clicking all 27 field cells once each) both FAILED to
   clear the level, proving SOMETHING about these positions was
   structurally necessary — correct evidence, but the "commit invariant"
   INTERPRETATION of it was wrong (see item 11).
10. **The trigger-click infinite-loop bug** (found while live-testing
    level 5, unrelated to whether level 5's mechanic is ever solved): the
    adapter's own "nothing unsatisfied, try a trigger click" fallback
    judged trigger success by "did anything visibly change" — but on a
    board where an ordinary field-cell click is ALWAYS visibly effective
    (this level, exactly), that check can never distinguish a genuine
    reveal from routine activity, so it looped on the SAME cell forever
    (60+ identical clicks observed live, zero contradictions detected).
    **Fixed**: trigger success is now judged by a wholesale board-LAYOUT
    replacement (Jaccard overlap of candidate-region bboxes, colour-blind,
    below 0.5 = a reveal), plus a bounded distinct-cell trigger budget and
    a bounded per-level action budget so no board — solved or not — can
    grind indefinitely. Live-verified: identical 4/6 score, but total
    actions on the same 500-budget smoke dropped from 500 (exhausted) to
    195 (a clean 150-action bounded bail once level 5 proved unsolvable
    with the current rule).
11. **Level 5 SOLVED — the "commit glyphs" are stateful cross-toggle
    control buttons, not a new invariant** (Codex,
    `docs/r58_codex_ft09_l4_solution_20260715.md`, committed `df12717`,
    resolving item 9). The confirmed constraint-satisfaction rule (top of
    this section) holds COMPLETELY — nothing about it was wrong. Two
    things WERE wrong: (a) the "commit glyph" positions are ordinary
    STATEFUL BUTTONS whose click toggles two things at once — their own
    colour 14<->15, AND every existing position painted with the ink-6
    "action stencil" around them (their own N/W/E/S neighbours) — this is
    a genuine mechanic, not a constraint-satisfaction rule at all, so it
    was never going to fit the ink-0/2/3 vocabulary; and (b) discovery's
    minimum-member floor (4) silently REJECTED two real target glyphs
    that only have 3 members each (`glyph@(4,14)` and `glyph@(52,46)`),
    which — together with the already-known `glyph@(36,22)` — constrain
    the 3 control buttons themselves via perfectly ordinary ink-2
    ("must differ from marker") constraints requiring each to reach
    colour 15. Gold's 21 clicks resolve exactly into 9 real target
    clicks + 9 "compensation" clicks (cells the control buttons' cross-
    toggle side effects pushed to 15, which then need a second click back
    to 14 to satisfy their OWN separate constraint) + 3 control-button
    clicks (each control needs exactly one click to satisfy its own
    ink-2 constraint). One field cell, `(20,22)`, is touched by TWO
    different controls' action stencils and cancels out automatically —
    genuinely unconstrained, the only one of 30 stateful board positions
    that is. Click order is NOT load-bearing (Codex verified gold order,
    full reverse order, and all-controls-first all clear on click 21).
    **Level 5's apparent decoy->reveal (item 5) was ALSO not a real
    mechanic**: the game engine defers level installation until the NEXT
    submitted action (visible in the vendored `arcengine` package's
    `base_game.py`), so the "reveal" click is simply the engine finishing
    installing level 5's board and rendering it before processing that
    same click — not a hidden second board being revealed by player
    action the way levels 2 and 6 genuinely are.

## Notes / Refactor Plan

- The historical `strat_lights_out` (sprite-tag reads) is fully retired by
  the current adapter and not called anywhere in the live path.
- **Level 5's mechanic is SOLVED (falsification-journey item 11) but NOT
  yet re-integrated into the adapter or re-smoked.** Concrete adapter work
  needed: (1) lower or otherwise fix the discovery minimum-member floor
  so genuine 3-member glyphs aren't silently dropped (the current `>=4`
  floor was tuned against level 6's truncated rings, which happened to
  both have exactly 4 members — level 5 proves 3-member glyphs are also
  real and load-bearing, so the floor needs to come down or be replaced
  with a better noise filter); (2) add the stateful control-button
  transition model (click toggles self + every existing ink-6 neighbour)
  as a NEW, board-discovered mechanic alongside the existing coupling
  model, not a constraint-vocabulary extension; (3) do NOT extend the
  decoy->reveal detection to treat level 5's level-start transition as a
  real reveal — it isn't one (see item 11). After these land, re-smoke
  2x500 to confirm level 5 clears and check whether level 6 becomes
  reachable in the same run (a "free bonus" if level 5's real click count
  is short enough to leave budget).
- Level 6 needs no further RESEARCH — its mechanic is fully decoded — but
  it has never been exercised live because level 5 blocks it sequentially.
  Once level 5 clears, re-smoke to see whether level 6 also clears as
  predicted.
