## Observations

- Cardinal movement grid
- BFS over state-space solves L1 on both v1 and v2
- Deeper levels likely introduce new mechanics
- script25 characterization (2026-07-15, no adapter built yet): the
  moving piece (colour9) shifts a CONSTANT (0,+4) pixel delta per
  ACTION4 press -- fixed-step, not sliding, confirmed both from gold
  (`data/traces/sp80.npz`) and a live single-episode probe. ACTION5
  does not move the piece; it recolours it IN PLACE (colour9 ->
  colour8) and THAT recolour is what flips `levels_completed`, not
  arrival at any particular cell. Gold's entire level-0 solve is
  exactly `[move, move, move, transform]` (4 actions,
  `min_actions_total: 4` in the trace meta).

## Mechanics Hypothesis

NOT a pure reach-the-goal navigation puzzle, despite the BFS-clears-L1
history above (BFS's blind search happens to find the transform action
too, so a working L1 clear was never evidence of a pure-movement
mechanic). This is a MOVE-THEN-TRANSFORM game: fixed-step cardinal
movement (matches the dc22-pattern movement family) positions a piece,
and a dedicated transform action commits/consumes it; level clearance
tracks transformed pieces, not player position.

**Falsified sub-hypothesis: transform requires the piece to be
delivered onto/near a target region.** Two static colour11 regions
(bbox `(52,16,59,27)` and `(52,40,59,51)`, 80 cells each) are present
throughout level 0 and looked target-slot-like (matches the T3/T4
"deliver to target" family, e.g. [[SB26]]'s portal-sort). Directly
checked against every frame in the gold trace: BOTH colour11 regions
are byte-identical in every single frame, before and after the
transform -- no appearance change, no "slot consumed" signal -- and the
transform itself happened at bbox `(16,24,19,43)`, rows 16-19, nowhere
near either colour11 region's rows 52-59. Position relative to colour11
is NOT the win-condition mechanism, at least for level 0.

**Also falsified: per-level PIECE COUNT.** A live probe pushed into
level 1 (no gold data covers this level) found a colour8 piece already
present at level start, plus relocated colour11 regions. Moving a
fresh colour9 piece and transforming it did NOT clear level 1. Follow-up
probe (300 actions, 149 transforms total, transforming every colour9
piece the moment it appeared, no movement) settled into a STABLE
4-transform REPEATING CYCLE -- colour8 region count oscillating
3,3,2,2 then a GAME_OVER, reset, and the identical cycle recurring --
`levels_completed` never budged past 1 across the whole run. Transform
count is not the win-condition mechanism either; something is being
CONSUMED/overwritten each cycle (colour8 count isn't monotonically
increasing), suggesting a maintained-configuration or specific-sequence
requirement rather than an accumulate-and-count one. Two hypotheses
(position-delivery, transform-count) are now cleanly falsified with
live evidence; the actual win condition is still open. Next step for
whoever picks this back up: characterize what's being consumed each
cycle (which specific colour8 region vanishes, and whether a fixed
SEQUENCE of transform positions/order -- not just count -- is required).

## Notes

Frame-only via BFS — generalizes on L1, but BFS finding the transform
action is not the same as BFS understanding the mechanic; a script25
adapter needs the move+transform(+count) model above, not a pure
navigation planner. Deeper levels may need heuristic pruning either way.

## Lessons Learned

- Frame-only solver generalizes across version hashes. See linked lessons for why frame-observation strategies are preferred.
- [[../lessons/frame_diff_as_probe]]
- A game with a working single-level BFS clear can still hide a
  non-navigation mechanic: SP80's L1 clear was assumed to be evidence
  of "pure movement puzzle" until a script25 gold-trace + live-probe
  characterization (2026-07-15) found a dedicated recolour/transform
  action was the actual win trigger, and a plausible-looking
  deliver-to-target sub-hypothesis (matching two static colour11
  regions) was directly falsified against the gold trace's own frames.
  Don't infer full mechanic understanding from one strategy's partial
  success.
