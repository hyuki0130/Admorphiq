# SP80 — Water-Routing / Spill-Coverage Puzzle

> Place deflector/block pieces during a CHANGE phase, then a SPILL phase drops water from a fixed source; a level wins only when the flow covers EVERY target region without hitting a hazard — NOT a reach-the-goal maze.

## Current Status

The R56 flow-kernel rebuild (`script25` quarantined adapter) now **clears L0
in 10 actions with efficiency score 1.0** (beats the 39-action human
baseline → per-level cap), lifting game_score from **0.000638 → 0.0476**
(2026-07-15, `scripts/rounds/script25_sp80_flowN`). game_score 0.0476 is the
completion cap for clearing only L0 of 6 (weight 1 of Σ1..6 = 21), so the
lever from here is DEPTH, not L0 efficiency (already maxed).

This replaces the earlier blind transition-graph explorer, which reached the
same 1/6 depth but only by stumbling onto the winning layout (L0 in 337
actions, game_score 0.000638; legacy `strat_bfs_state_space` took 2058
actions). Blind search is BLIND to the water flow — the fix was to LEARN it.

Deeper levels (L1+) still fall back to graph exploration: they use multiple
pieces requiring targeted ACTION6 selection (an unbounded click space) and/or
angled deflectors, so the adapter is 1/6 at standard budgets, but with L0 now
efficient.

**Key observability finding**: the spill exposes its ENTIRE animation as the
observation's successive frame LAYERS (one layer per tick, water descending
one grid cell per layer) at the moment of commit. So a SINGLE sacrificial
spill reveals the whole trajectory — no tick-by-tick stepping needed — and a
failed spill returns to the change phase in ~1 tick, making the learning spill
cost ~2 actions and 1 of the 4 allowed attempts.

## Observations

- Two-phase controls (v2 board, background colour 12):
  - CHANGE phase: a movable BLOCK/DEFLECTOR piece (the selected one is
    recoloured — colour 9 on the live board, an 80-cell block), a fixed
    WATER SOURCE near the top (colour-4 emitter + colour-6 spout, ~col 36),
    and two TARGET regions (colour 11, 80 cells each, bboxes `(52,16,59,27)`
    and `(52,40,59,51)`).
  - HUD: a top row (colour 14) and a bottom band (colour 1, shared with the
    hazard colour) — both edge-pinned, masked before hashing.
- `ACTION1..4` move the selected piece one fixed cell (collision-checked).
- `ACTION5` commits the layout → spill phase. `ACTION6` clicks to select a
  different piece; the engine auto-selects the origin-nearest piece at level
  start and after each failed spill.
- Only 4 spill attempts, then GAME_OVER; a per-level step budget also loses.

## Mechanics Hypothesis

Water-routing coverage (confirmed from the game source, dev-time only): the
spill drops water from the source, splitting/deflecting off placed pieces;
each target that the settled flow covers is marked satisfied (recoloured to
13). WIN = every target satisfied AND no hazard hit. This is a
[[../concepts/merge_mechanic]]-adjacent coverage condition over a simulated
fluid flow, NOT navigation — which is why the two earlier hypotheses below
were correctly falsified.

**Falsified (2026-07-15, gold trace + live probe)**: (a) "transform requires
delivering the piece onto/near a target" — the two colour-11 regions are
byte-identical before/after the win, and the win fires nowhere near them;
(b) "the win is a transform COUNT" — a 149-transform run cycled 3,3,2,2 then
GAME_OVER without ever advancing. Both are consistent with the real
mechanic: it is the water FLOW reaching targets, not piece position or
transform count.

## Solution Pattern (frame-only — learned flow model)

Compose the fluid-flow motion kernels (`learn_flow_operators` /
`simulate_flow` / `plan_flow_coverage`, the fluid analogue of the
`learn_point_operators` / `plan_overwrites` and reflection pairs):

1. **Learn** — commit ONE sacrificial spill; read its stacked animation
   layers; `learn_flow_operators` recovers the fall direction (down) + the
   source emit cells from them.
2. **Probe** — measure the movable piece's per-action displacement (the
   cluster that rigidly translates under an ACTION1-4 probe is the piece).
3. **Detect roles** — the goal regions are the static clusters DOWNSTREAM of
   the source in the fall direction (this excludes the upstream emitter);
   the movable piece is the probed cluster.
4. **Plan** — `plan_flow_coverage` searches piece placements, simulating the
   flow (fall; spread perpendicular around the obstacle; interior-hit
   satisfies a target) for each, until one covers EVERY target; execute the
   moves and commit the winning spill.

Fallback: no flow model, no movable piece, or no covering placement (multi-
piece levels needing ACTION6 selection, angled deflectors) ⇒ the same generic
transition-graph frontier explorer the previous build used, so depth never
regresses below that baseline.

## Lessons Learned

- **The spill's whole trajectory is one multi-layer frame.** SP80 exposes the
  spill animation as the observation's successive frame LAYERS (one per tick)
  at commit — a single sacrificial spill reveals the entire flow. This
  observability is what makes learning the flow cheap (~2 actions).
- **The flow-around-obstacle rule is generic, not game physics.** Water falls,
  spreads perpendicular along an obstacle's face, and resumes falling off the
  edges — parameterised only by the learned fall direction. A pixel-space
  droplet sim of this matched the engine's WIN/lose on all 7 tested L0
  placements.
- **Downstream filtering separates targets from the emitter.** The source
  emitter is a static cluster too; excluding clusters upstream of the source
  (opposite the fall direction) keeps it from being mistaken for a target the
  flow can never cover.
- A working single-level BFS clear can hide a non-navigation mechanic:
  SP80's L0 clear was assumed to be a movement puzzle until the source read
  + gold/live characterization found a water-routing coverage win. Don't
  infer full mechanic understanding from one strategy's partial success.

  from frames without internals
