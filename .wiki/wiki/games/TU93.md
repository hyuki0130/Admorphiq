## Slide-kernel validation (task #50, 2026-07-15) — BANKED

A generic slide successor kernel was built and VALIDATED against TU93's own
transitions (validate-first): `slide_endpoint(passable, start, direction,
stop)` in `src/admorphiq/kernels/paths.py` (stop rules as data: `until_wall`,
`until_bend`, `until_adjacent_obstacle`; plus `slide_chain` for momentum
shoves). Measured on 114+ live level-0 transitions with a pixel floor
passability and per-action direction calibration: **~28% reproduction**
(`until_wall` and `until_bend` identical) — only marginally above the earlier
banked tile-graph plateau (21%), and STRIKINGLY one action (down) reproduces
at 90% while the other three fail at any direction.

Conclusion (BANK): TU93's slide is a CORRIDOR-FOLLOWING slide — the avatar
rolls along a bending corridor and lands OFF the straight ray from its start.
A straight-line `slide_endpoint` fundamentally cannot model that (the endpoint
isn't on the launch ray), which is exactly why the banked corridor-prediction
plateaued: the residual is the connectivity/path model, not the stop rule. The
generic slide kernel is delivered and reusable for TRUE straight ice-slide +
momentum-push games (validated on synthetic lanes), but was deliberately NOT
wired into TU93 (it doesn't reproduce the mechanic — forcing it would regress
nothing but add a wrong model). The real lever remains a corridor-path-
following successor over a corrected floor-connectivity graph — a distinct
follow-up, banked honestly. TU93 stays on its transition-graph adapter (2/9).

### Follow-up: "fit passability to observed traversals" tested — also caps out (2026-07-15)

The proposed fix (rebuild the floor-connectivity graph from what the avatar
ACTUALLY traverses, rather than pixel colours) was measured rigorously:
- A randomized exploration collected 898 moving level-0 transitions across 77
  unique cells; the per-action direction resolved cleanly to CARDINAL
  (1=up, 2=down, 3=left, 4=right) once the sample was diverse (a degenerate
  cyclic probe that trapped the avatar in 6 cells had earlier given a
  misleading 61% and a corrupted 2-actions-both-up map — a sampling artifact).
- Passability fitted from observed endpoint cells + the straight lane between
  each from/to, then `slide_endpoint` scored on an 80/20 split:
  **train 11%, HELD-OUT 16%** (`until_wall`; `until_bend` worse at 4%).

Root cause (confirmed): the slide is CORRIDOR-BENDING — under a cardinal
action the avatar rolls along a corridor that turns, landing at a column/row
the launch axis never predicts. Two consequences: (a) a straight
`slide_endpoint` cannot express an off-ray endpoint; (b) endpoint-fitted
passability cannot recover the true corridor FLOOR (the cells a bend passes
through are unobservable from endpoints, and the straight-lane interpolation
cuts through walls, corrupting the mask). So "fit passability to traversals"
does NOT rescue a bending slide — it needs the full bent PATH per (cell,
action), which is exactly what the transition-graph adapter already learns by
trial. The remaining tu93 lever is therefore EXPLORATION EFFICIENCY
(goal-directed frontier expansion to connect the graph in fewer actions), not
a predictive slide/connectivity model — the slide-model avenue is closed with
data (16% held-out ≪ the >50% a wire-worthy model needs).

## Exploration efficiency: goal-directed frontier (2026-07-15) — SHIPPED, 11.6×

The remaining lever the slide-model bank pointed at (EXPLORATION EFFICIENCY, not
slide prediction) was implemented and measured. The transition-graph adapter's
blind frontier expansion (`_nearest_untried` returned the graph-hop-NEAREST
untried cell, ignoring the goal) explored nearly the whole maze away from the
exit — a diagnostic showed L1 probing **113 unique cells / 94 self-loops** to
reach a goal at (45,45). Two frame-only, no-new-state changes make exploration
goal-directed:

1. **Goal-proximity frontier selection** (`_nearest_untried`): the current cell
   still wins when it has an untried action (a free probe, no walk), but of every
   reachable untried-frontier cell the adapter now expands the one CLOSEST TO THE
   GOAL by Manhattan distance (the goal is frame-identifiable via `_detect_goal`),
   tie-broken by graph-hop nearness.
2. **Goal-directed action ordering** (`_goal_ward_order` / `_learned_action_dirs`):
   each action id's MEAN observed (dr,dc) over the transitions already recorded is
   a stable "which way does this action push" signal (the slide isn't a fixed
   vector, but its mean direction is). Untried actions are probed in order of how
   well that mean points toward the goal; actions with no learned direction yet
   keep their place after the ranked ones. This is used ONLY to order probes —
   never to predict a landing cell (that avenue is closed, see above).

**Measured @3000, deterministic ×2** (`scripts/script25.py --games tu93
--max-actions 3000`):

| | L1 actions | L2 actions | game_score |
|---|---|---|---|
| blind frontier (before) | 637 | 226 | 0.000243 |
| goal-directed (after) | **249** | **65** | **0.002822** |

Floor preserved (still 2/9, no level regressed), L1 −61%, L2 −71%, game_score
**11.6×**. L2's per-level score reached 0.061 (65 actions vs human 16).

**Residual is structural, banked.** L1 at 249 is still ≫ the 19-action human: the
avatar must PROBE to learn each slide's outcome (the slide is corridor-bending and
unpredictable from the frame — the closed slide-model bank), so it can never match
pure informed navigation. Goal-direction cuts HOW MANY cells get probed before the
goal connects; it cannot remove probing itself. Reaching near-human here would
require a predictive slide/connectivity model, which measured at 16% held-out
(≪ wire-worthy) — so this is the efficiency ceiling for the graph approach, and
0.0028 is banked as that ceiling. L3+ still does not connect within 3000 (a larger
maze); the prize was L1/L2 efficiency, achieved.

## Efficiency re-audit (R62, 2026-07-16) — where the actions ACTUALLY go: sink is on the UNSCORED level; scored levels are at the probing floor

Re-opened as a dedicated efficiency round (target: cut the two cleared levels'
action count). A per-action-KIND breakdown (classifying each action as
identity / detect / probe-here / walk-to-frontier / route-to-goal, plus GAME_OVER
fuses) was measured on the live run (env tu93-0768757b, @3000, deterministic ×2 =
2/9 @ 0.0028; scored L1=249 vs human 19, L2=65 vs human 16). **Verdict: no cheap
scored-level win exists — 0.0028 is confirmed at the graph ceiling. The "thousands
of actions" the round premise cited are on the UNCLEARED L3, which does not score.**

Measured breakdown (level index = `levels_completed` at the action):

| level (role) | total actions | GAME_OVER fuses | probe-here | walk-to-frontier | scores? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (→ scored L1) | 287 | 5 | 227 | 51 | yes (249) |
| 1 (→ scored L2) | 62 | 1 | 61 | 1 | yes (65) |
| 2 (UNCLEARED L3) | 2325 | **320** | 101 | **1904** | **no** |

- **The scored-level cost is irreducible slide-PROBING.** Scored L1 = ~227 goal-ward
  probes + ~51 post-GAME_OVER re-walks; scored L2 = ~61 probes. Each probe learns
  ONE `(cell, action)→cell` slide edge by trial; slides are corridor-bending and
  frame-unpredictable (slide-landing prediction plateaued at 16% held-out — banked
  above), so probing cannot be replaced by prediction, and the goal-directed
  frontier (shipped R56) already minimises off-path probing.
- **The re-walks are already optimal.** After each GAME_OVER fuse the avatar resets
  to start and re-walks to the frontier via `transition_shortest_path` (shortest
  path over the known graph); the 51 L1 walks ≈ the 5 post-fuse re-walks — necessary,
  not oscillation. Committing to a frontier target (the obvious micro-lever) would
  not remove them.
- **The apparent waste is the UNSCORED L3.** 1904 walk-to-frontier + 320 GAME_OVER
  fuses = the bulk of the 3000 budget, spent trying (and failing) to connect L3's
  larger maze. It never clears at @3000, so it contributes 0 to game_score — the
  "~4120 actions" framing counts this unscored exploration, not the 249+65 scored.

**The round's cheap-fix hypotheses, tested against ground truth:** (a) "re-explores
from scratch per level" — NO, the graph correctly resets per level (each level is a
new maze); per-level probing is necessary first-time exploration, not redundant
re-exploration. (b) "wanders before locking the goal" — the SCORED-level wandering
is minor (L1 51 ≈ post-fuse re-walks, L2 1); the massive wander (1904) is on the
unscored L3. (c) "cut the discovery stride" — the stride IS per-cell slide-probing;
each probe learns a real unpredictable edge, so it cannot be strided without the
closed slide-prediction model.

**Score-lever arithmetic (why even L2 is not a cheap target).** game_score =
(1·s_L1 + 2·s_L2)/45 with s=(human/agent)². L2 (weight 2) dominates, so halving
L2 65→32 would ~4× the score — but L2's 65 is ~61 slide-probes of the SAME
irreducible kind (61 probes for a 16-slide solution; the maze is smaller so it
connects sooner than L1). No config/heuristic tweak cuts it without the 16%-closed
slide model. Per the sprint's "⛔ no more config sweeps hoping they flip" rule and
the R56 slide-model bank, this avenue stays closed.

**Closed for the sprint at the graph ceiling.** The only paths to more tu93 score
are (a) a predictive slide/connectivity model (measured 16% held-out ≪ wire-worthy,
banked) or (b) clearing L3+ (a NEW-LEVEL lever, explicitly out of this round's
scope). Floor 2/9 @ 0.0028 held (no code change, deterministic ×2, env tu93-0768757b,
@3000). Bench dir: `scripts/rounds/script25_tu93_eff/`.

## Lessons Learned

- Current solver is brittle — v1 works, v2 fails. See the linked lessons for the underlying pattern and refactor path.
- **A straight-slide kernel ≠ a corridor-following slide.** Validate-first
  measurement (~28% reproduction) confirmed TU93's slide bends through
  corridors, landing off the launch ray — a straight ray-cast can't express
  it. Bank when the model can't reproduce the measured endpoints.
