# CN04

> A rigid-arrangement puzzle: select a sprite, rotate and slide it so its connector-marker stubs coincide with another sprite's — NOT a click game.

## Current Status

- **v2** (`cn04-2fe56bfb`, only local env): **level 1 CLEARED generically** by
  `src/admorphiq/adapters25/cn04.py` (R56 script25), ~36 actions,
  `game_score` 0.031 on the 6-level RHAE. Levels 2+ banked (see Refactor Plan).
- The historical `zig3_A2A4` "click" reading (1/5 on v1) was a
  MISCLASSIFICATION: it blind-zig-zagged and happened to align level-1 markers
  on the v1 hash; it scores 0 on v2 and does not describe the mechanic.

## Observations

Measured from the live v2 frame + the game source (dev-time reading only; the
adapter acts frame-only):

- One sprite is ACTIVE; the engine renders its body as **colour 0**. Every
  other visible sprite keeps a **native colour** (12/14/11/9…).
- **ACTION6** on a sprite makes it active (the previous active reverts to its
  native colour). **ACTION1-4** translate the active sprite one grid cell
  (up/down/left/right — sign measured, not assumed). **ACTION5** rotates it 90°.
- Every sprite carries connector-marker **stubs rendered as colour 8**. When a
  stub lands on the same grid cell as another sprite's stub, both recolour to
  **3**. The level wins when NO colour-8 stub remains (all satisfied).
- **Row 0 is a HUD** step-countdown bar drawn in colours 0 and 4 — must be
  masked before reading "colour 0 = active body".
- 1 grid cell = 3 display px (measured from a single move); each stub is a 3×3
  block.

## Mechanics Hypothesis

Arrange pieces so their connector stubs interlock. For a piece with a
2-stub LINE, the active piece's stub line must be rotated to match the target
line's orientation, then translated so the stubs coincide exactly. The win
check pairs stubs by a HIDDEN original colour (source colours 8 and 13, both
displayed as 8), so a geometric coincidence recolours to 3 yet may not win if
the hidden pairing is swapped — the two 180°-opposite orientations of a line
look identical but only one wins. See [[../concepts/rotation_state]].

## Solution Pattern

The R56 adapter (`adapters25/cn04.py`) composes only
`kernels.find_regions` for perception; all roles/plan are declared in the
adapter:

1. Mask row-0 HUD; segment regions. Active = colour-0 body; markers = colour-8.
2. Lock the target stubs once (distant colour-8 stubs, by bbox distance from
   the active body) — they are static, so this survives later occlusion.
3. Rotate (ACTION5) until the active stub line's translation-invariant shape
   matches the target line's; latch the marker positions as fixed offsets from
   the always-visible body centroid.
4. Distance-hill-climb: take the measured unit move that most reduces the
   sorted marker→target Manhattan cost until exact overlap.
5. If every stub coincides (colour-8 count 0, colour-3 present) but NO WIN, the
   hidden pairing is swapped → RESET the level and pre-rotate two extra
   quarter-turns (180° endpoint swap), then re-solve. Two attempts cover both
   chiralities.

## Notes / Refactor Plan (banked walls)

- **Level 2+ — BANKED.** Level 2 has 4 sprites / ~12 stubs (multiple pairs).
  The adapter models ONE active line vs ONE target line and has no global
  stub-pairing/assignment, so it thrashes. **Reopen** with a global pairing
  step (`kernels.assign_pairs` over stub shape/adjacency to fix the
  correspondence, then solve pieces in sequence).
- **Levels 3-6 — grey masking** hides non-active sprites and their stubs until
  selected. **Reopen** with a select-and-probe memory: click each sprite once
  to reveal and cache its stub positions before planning.
- Both are research increments, not tuning; the single-pair spine here is the
  prerequisite. No hardcoded coordinates/sequences were added.

## Lessons Learned

- A game's historical "type" tag can be wrong: CN04 was tagged `click` because
  a blind action pattern happened to solve one hash's level 1. Re-derive the
  mechanic from frames, don't trust the tag. See [[../lessons/frame_diff_as_probe]].
- Occlusion matters: the active body (drawn on top) hides the target stubs on
  approach, so track your own markers as a fixed offset from the always-visible
  body, not by re-reading colour-8 each frame.
- When the win rule keys on a colour the frame can't distinguish, use the
  engine's own WIN signal as ground truth and retry the alternative
  orientation on a no-win coincidence.
