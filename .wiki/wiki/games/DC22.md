## Observations

- Single-layer grid, movement game
- ACTION1-4 cardinal; ACTION6 can toggle barriers by clicking buttons
- `button_click_move`: click buttons first, then navigate the exit path

## Mechanics Hypothesis

Hybrid button + movement: certain buttons in the grid toggle barrier states; the player must click-toggle the right buttons and then walk to the exit.

## Solution Pattern (frame-only — gated-maze product-graph planner, 2026-07-15)

The R56 rebuild (`script25` adapter) clears L0 GENERICALLY via
`plan_gated_path`: **1/6, L0 in 78 actions (human 59, score 0.572)**, up from
game_score 0.0. This is the validated FOURTH plan after three falsified ones
(reactive stuck-probe / parity-enumeration / decoupled set-state-then-walk —
see Lessons).

Validated model (gold L0 replays to WIN, verified live): DC22 is a
PRODUCT-GRAPH gated maze. A cell is a WALL iff its colour is a learned wall
colour (colour 0 unrevealed + colour 4 closed barrier — learned from blocked
moves, never hardcoded). Each toggle button mutates the wall-cell SET:
- a REVEAL removes a fixed set permanently (one-time; measured colour 0→5);
- a SEESAW / BARRIER toggles a fixed set's wall-membership each click (a
  symmetric-difference; measured colour 4↔8 / 4↔9).
A toggle is classified by clicking it TWICE: cells changed on BOTH = the swap
set, cells changed only on the FIRST = the one-time reveal. Win = reach the
goal cell in SOME reachable `(cell, wall_set)` state.

The adapter learns avatar/dir_map/goal/wall-colours (optimistic walk-and-learn
bootstrap), discovers the two largest-diff toggle buttons (clicking each
twice), then composes `plan_gated_path` (BFS over `(cell, wall_set)`: move
edges gated by the wall set, toggle edges mutating it via reveal-removal /
seesaw symmetric-difference) to emit the interleaved walk+click plan and
executes it. The generic offline-validated plan is 20 actions (== gold); the
78 live actions include the button-discovery bootstrap overhead (each
candidate clicked twice) — a clear efficiency lever for a future pass.

### Efficiency pass BANKED (2026-07-15) — overhead is stuck-gated discovery, NOT click count

Target: L0 clears at 78 actions; the harness caps a per-level score at 1.0, so
ANY clear at **≤59 actions** (the human baseline) → score 1.0 → game_score
≈0.0476 (vs 0.0272 today). MEASURED breakdown of the 78: **57 bootstrap
(16 moves + 41 clicks) + ~20 gold-optimal plan** — the plan can't shrink, so
the ≥19-action cut must come from the bootstrap. Discovery curve: of ~26
clicked regions only **2** have a real footprint (a 128-cell REVEAL + a 16-cell
SEESAW); the other ~24 are 1-cell cosmetic indicator flips. Both real togglers
are first-clicked by ~step 9.

Three configs were built + measured (@1000), NONE reached ≤59:
- **One-click classification** (classify reveal-vs-seesaw from a single click's
  colour transitions — opened=wall→non-wall, closed=reverse): **0/6, L0
  BROKE.** One-click classification is wall-colour-DEPENDENT (the seesaw can't
  be told from a reveal until colour 4 is a known wall colour), and classifying
  eagerly at click time froze a wrong verdict that never recovered. A lazy
  re-classification fix (store transitions, resolve against the latest palette)
  restored the clear but at **93 actions — WORSE than 78**, because the
  wall-independent two-click method classified earlier than the palette-gated
  one-click method could.
- **Prune cosmetics** (keep two-click classification, but a click counts as a
  toggler only if its HUD-masked diff ≥3 cells, so the ~24 one-cell decoys
  never qualify or get second-clicked): **77 actions — a 1-action improvement,
  negligible.**
- **Flip-rate cosmetic mask** (2026-07-15, from a code-grounded design review):
  keep two-click classification, but mask any cell that changes under ≥80% of
  ALL clicks — the click-space analogue of a move-rate HUD mask — and subtract
  it from every button's effective diff. Predicted to isolate a single
  per-click indicator pixel and drop the bootstrap to ~17. **MEASURED: 78
  actions — byte-identical to baseline, i.e. a NO-OP.** The mask is EMPTY: no
  single cell flips on ≥80% of clicks, which means the ~24 one-cell decoy diffs
  are **DISTINCT cells, not one shared indicator pixel**. The "cosmetic pixel
  flips on every click" premise does not hold for this env — each decoy region's
  click changes its own local cell. Reverted.

**Root cause (why click-count levers barely move it): the bootstrap only probes
a button when its optimistic walk is STUCK, then walks many steps before the
next stuck.** So the two real togglers don't both reach two-click classification
until ~step 55 regardless of how the cosmetic decoys are filtered — the cost is
the stuck-gated discovery interleaved with optimistic walking, not the number
of clicks. Removing the ~13 cosmetic second-clicks did nothing because the plan
still waits on the two significant togglers classifying late.

**Reopen pointer**: decouple significant-button classification from the stuck
gate — when a ≥3-cell-diff button is discovered, complete its second click (and
keep discovering) EAGERLY rather than only when next stuck, so both real
togglers classify within the first ~15 actions and the plan builds early. That
is an architectural change to the bootstrap loop (regression risk — the
one-click attempt already broke L0), out of scope for a bounded pass. Reverted
to the committed 78-action baseline. Bench dirs: `scripts/rounds/script25_dc22_eff*`.

## L1 investigation (R59, 2026-07-16) — DETECTION works, PLANNING fails (goal unreachable in-model)

Bounded investigation pass on L1 (the wall; DC22 holds 1/6 @ 0.0272, floor). Verdict: **detection is fine, PLANNING is the wall.**

- **Detection OK.** On L1 the adapter finds the avatar (colour 14), the goal, learns a wall colour (``_wall_colors={4}``), and discovers buttons (up to 27) — exactly as on L0. No detection gate fails.
- **Planning fails.** ``_can_plan()`` becomes True by ~L1 action 96 (≥2 classified togglers, dir_map learned), but ``_build_gated_plan`` / ``plan_gated_path`` returns an EMPTY/None plan on EVERY call thereafter (measured at 2, 3, …, 14 classified togglers) — the goal is UNREACHABLE in the adapter's learned ``(cell, wall-set)`` product graph. Since ``if plan:`` treats an empty list as no-plan, ``self._plan`` stays None and the adapter loops the bootstrap forever (``blocked`` plateaus at 116 cells), never clearing L1. So this is NOT a toggler-count problem (all togglers classified still yields no path) — the model itself has no route to the goal.
- **Strong lead for WHY (not yet root-caused — needs an engine-ground-truth comparison).** L1's DOMINANT / background colour FLIPS from colour 4 (L0) to colour 5 (L1); colour 4 is still present (~1256 px ≈ 50 cells) but is no longer the background. The adapter learns ``_wall_colors={4}`` from blocked moves and then walls EVERY colour-4 cell in ``_build_gated_plan`` (``walls0 = all cells whose colour ∈ _wall_colors``). On the flipped-palette L1 this blanket colour-4 walling likely over-restricts passability (and/or the toggle reveal/swap sets, captured against the flipped palette, don't open the real path), enclosing the goal in the model. A frame-only reachability check here was inconclusive (the probe mixed pixel vs 5-cell coordinates — the adapter navigates by measured dir_map deltas, not a fixed 5-grid), so the over-restriction vs new-mechanic question is open.
- **Why not a small fix this pass:** the wiki's own L0 efficiency history shows eager wall/toggle-model changes BROKE L0 (one-click classification 0/6); a wall-model change must be validated against L0 byte-identical + the L1 engine ground truth before shipping. That validation (map the engine's true L1 passability + real toggle sets, confirm the adapter's blanket colour-4 walling is the specific over-restriction, fix it gated on the L1 signature) is the focused continuation — a small but validation-heavy change, out of scope for a bounded investigation pass. Floor 1/6 @ 0.0272 held (no adapter change). Measurement: scratch probes this session (detection OK; plan_gated_path empty at 2–14 togglers).

## Lessons Learned

- **Three falsified plans located the wall precisely; the 4th (product graph)
  cleared it.** DC22's win is neither reachability-triggered (reactive
  stuck-probe never fires — gold's path is never physically blocked because it
  toggles just-in-time), nor a proactive flag (decoupled set-state-then-walk
  hits the ~128-action fuse), nor parity-enumerable (a one-time REVEAL is
  distinct from click-parity). It is a product graph over `(position,
  wall-set)` where clicks mutate the wall set — walk and toggles inseparable.
- **Represent the toggle state as the WALL-CELL SET, not parity bits.** Toggle
  effects are colour SWAPS that interact when cell sets overlap; a parity-bit
  model mis-predicts one cell. Encoding the layer as the current wall
  frozenset and mutating via symmetric-difference simulates the swaps exactly,
  regardless of order — this was the difference between a plan that reaches the
  goal in-model but blocks live (step 12) and one that WINS.

- Frame-only solver generalizes across version hashes. See linked lessons for why frame-observation strategies are preferred.
- [[../lessons/frame_diff_as_probe]]
- The current graph-search agent (`--agent unified`/`chained`) still clears
  0/6 here (R53, 2026-07-13) — the confining starting box the player begins
  in trips the SAME region-mask heuristic meant for HUD widgets (it responds
  to every movement key, same as a step counter would), blinding the graph
  to different in-box positions. Two candidate fixes were measured live and
  both falsified; see
  [[../lessons/dc22_confined_avatar_discriminator_falsified_20260713]]
  before re-attempting a discriminator for this.
- **script25 adapter built and live-probed (R56, 2026-07-15, commit
  `0e59f88`, `src/admorphiq/adapters25/dc22.py`).** A DIFFERENT agent
  family from the graph-search one above — identifies the avatar by direct
  movement measurement, never by the generic HUD/avatar region-mask
  discriminator, so it does not encounter the confining-starting-box
  failure at all. Reuses ka59's optimistic-passability navigation design;
  a measured seesaw button (opening one path while closing a different
  one) is handled by dropping any cell that changes after a probe click
  from `_known_blocked`, sidestepping the need to interpret button
  semantics. Live probe: 0/6, but avatar/goal identification, `dir_map`,
  and click-effect measurement are all individually verified correct — the
  wall is a probe-SEMANTICS gap, not a plumbing gap, with two named parts:
  cosmetic indicator-flip cells over-counted as genuine barrier changes
  (still open), and a never-re-probe rule that incorrectly forbade the
  gold trace's genuine seesaw re-visit. The second part was FIXED same
  session (per-button click memory + cosmetic-signature subtraction +
  stuck-state toggler cycling + route-proximity candidate ranking) and
  re-measured live 6 times — still 0/6, a genuine negative result on that
  gap specifically, not an unmeasured fix. See
  [[../rounds/r56_generic-kernels]]'s "Measured so far (continued) — dc22"
  section for the full writeup.
- **BANKED at 0/6, same session — the wall is architectural, precisely
  located via gold-replay divergence.** Replaying gold's real recorded
  20-action level-0 win (frame-before/frame-after pairs, not re-simulated)
  through the adapter's own decision logic found the decisive fact:
  `known_blocked` stays at ZERO cells for ALL 20 of gold's actions — gold's
  path never fails a single move, start to WIN. The adapter's probe tier
  only ever fires when the optimistic walk is genuinely blocked, so on
  gold's own path it can never click anything, ever — not a mistimed
  trigger, a structurally absent one. Gold's two real buttons sit 30-40
  cells from wherever the avatar happens to be at click time, confirming
  ACTION6 is a free screen-coordinate click, not proximity-gated, and that
  the buttons gate something checked at WIN (a state/flag), not the
  avatar's walkable path. Built a parity-combo enumeration policy in
  response (lock in the two largest-footprint known togglers, cycle all 4
  click-parity combos, no walking needed mid-combo since clicks are
  position-free) — one iteration found and fixed a premature-lock-in bug
  (locking in as soon as 2 togglers existed grabbed small nearby decorative
  regions discovered first, not gold's real far-away buttons; fixed by
  deferring lock-in to the first time the avatar actually reaches the goal
  cell, by which point ordinary discovery has had the whole journey to
  build a richer pool). Still 0/6 after the fix: in the measured 500-action
  live run, the avatar's ordinary walk/stuck/discovery loop never once
  reaches the goal cell at all, spending its budget re-cycling two small
  nearby decorative buttons via stuck-state toggling instead of making
  forward progress — so parity-combo enumeration never even got a chance
  to start. Conclusion: this level's real mechanic (a proactive, state-
  gated win check decoupled from physical navigation) sits outside what a
  reactive walk-stuck-probe architecture can express; re-attempting this
  game needs a different navigation/goal-detection design, not another
  probe-priority heuristic. Full technical detail in
  `src/admorphiq/adapters25/dc22.py`'s module docstring.
- **Proactive state-gate plan ALSO falsified (R56 follow-up, validate-before-
  build).** Tried the "detect the win-gate, satisfy it proactively, then walk
  to the goal" restructure. Gold's L0 win (verified to replay to WIN live) is
  20 actions that INTERLEAVE walk and toggle — click A (48,36; 97-cell one-time
  REVEAL), walk, click B (44,20; 49-cell SEESAW), walk, click A again (gate
  flip), walk to goal. DECOUPLING was tested directly: setting gold's end
  button-state up front (A, or A+B, or A+B+A) then walking greedily to the goal
  NEVER reaches it — every variant GAME_OVERs at the ~128-action per-life fuse
  far from the goal, because B's seesaw gates different path SEGMENTS that must
  be toggled at specific points along the route. The parity enumeration also
  can't represent gold's winning state (A clicked TWICE = even parity but its
  one-time reveal IS triggered; parity-0 == 0-clicks == no-reveal in the
  4-combo space). Focusing the stuck-probe on the largest-diff toggler was
  measured too — still 0/6. So dc22 L0 is an INTERLEAVED walk+toggle sequence
  (segment-by-segment: walk to a sub-goal, toggle the segment gate, walk on)
  under a tight fuse — reopen with a sequential-subgoal planner that tracks
  one-time reveals separately from parity, NOT a state-gate enumerator. Three
  plans now falsified (reactive stuck-probe / parity-enum / decoupled
  state-gate).
