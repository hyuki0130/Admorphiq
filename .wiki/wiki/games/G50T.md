## L1 ROOT CAUSE FOUND (2026-07-18, task #83) — the "offset instability" is a CAMERA-LOCK SCROLLING view, NOT a static-origin anchoring problem; both prescribed landmark strategies are INAPPLICABLE. Banked at 1/7 @ 0.0357 (L0 byte-identical, zero src/ change)

The anchoring build was chartered to "derive the 6px grid origin from a STATIC
landmark (floor-bbox extremes, fallback the static colour-9 goal blob) and
validate the spawn cell parses identically ×3". That premise is FALSIFIED: **on
L1 there is no static landmark because the frame is a camera that LOCKS onto the
player and scrolls the whole world underneath it.** Decisive measurements
(`scratchpad/g50t_l1_scroll.py`, `g50t_l1_anchor3.py`, `g50t_l1_anchor4.py`):

- **Pristine spawn parse IS stable** ×3 = frame cell **(8,6)** (2-cell mover),
  floor set size 25 — so anchor3's "capture spawn at the first L1 frame" fix
  worked. But (8,6) is a **pre-camera-lock artifact**, useless for navigation.
- **The FIRST displacing move snaps the camera**: player jumps (8,6)→**(3,4)**,
  the floor bbox shifts `(0,-6)` (one cell) and the whole floor fingerprint
  changes (symdiff 25/25). After that snap the **player is LOCKED at screen cell
  (3,4) for every subsequent move in every direction** (verified across a 16-move
  varied walk: player == (3,4) at every step). The world scrolls; the player does
  not move on screen.
- **The engine-cell deltas do NOT transfer to frame space.** Frame spawn is at
  row 8 (bottom of the crop); `plateA = spawn + Δ(2,-6)` lands at row 10, BELOW
  the HUD-cropped play area — impossible. The frame is a scrolled/re-centred view,
  not the engine grid, so the spawn-relative-delta scheme (task #82 brief Q3) is
  also dead as written.
- **Frame-native barrier-slide plate discovery is confounded by the scroll**
  (`anchor4.py`): every attempted target reports the SAME `added/removed` floor
  set (the scroll delta, not a real barrier slide) and the player never arrives
  where BFS intended — because the world moved under it.

**The one genuinely usable signal that DID emerge — a clean move/block detector:**
under camera-lock, a successful move scrolls the floor (`floor_symdiff > 0`), a
wall-blocked move leaves it unchanged (`floor_symdiff == 0`), lagged by the
uniform-2 obs delay. This is the correct foundation for the REAL (multi-session,
NOT bounded-pass) build.

- **THE crux (sharpened): correct action-attribution under the lag.** A reliable

  (`O == last_from`) FALSE-POSITIVED — the lag makes `O == last_from` appear for

  positives but the `+in-flight` estimate still phantomed onto the plate. The
  correct primitive: issue ONE move, wait until the observation provably reflects
  that move's result (2 reads) before advancing `real` or concluding a wall —
  a delayed-observation state estimator, a dedicated build, not a bounded tweak.

## L1 multi-ghost DECODE (2026-07-17) — ACTION5 supports N-1 CONCURRENT ghosts; L1 needs 2 + a perception fix (NOT built this pass)

Bounded pass on L1. The load-bearing question — does ACTION5 support two
concurrent ghosts, or does a second recording replace the first? — is **DECODED
(source-verified) and answered: ghosts ACCUMULATE, up to `N-1` concurrent, where
`N` = the number of `gpkhwmwioo` waypoint sprites on the level.** L1 has **3
waypoints → 2 concurrent ghosts** (L0 had 2 → 1, which is why L0 needed exactly
one). So the L1 plan shape is **two-ghost seating**, not sequential single-ghost
legs.

**Source mechanism** (`qxlodtievc`, `environment_files/g50t/5849a774/g50t.py`):
`rloltuowth` is a **dict** `{ghost: recorded_path}` (not a single slot). Each
ACTION5 (`pmlawcgvcp`) rewinds the current player's path to start, then in
`step()` increments the waypoint counter `rlazdofsxb` and, **if
`rlazdofsxb < len(drofvwhbxb)`**, BANKS the current player as a new ghost keyed
into `rloltuowth` (accumulate — prior ghosts untouched) and CLONES a fresh
player at start. When `rlazdofsxb == N` the Nth press instead CLEARS every ghost
and resets. So presses 1..N-1 each add a ghost; press N wipes. All ghosts replay
in LOCKSTEP with the current recording-phase move index (`move()` steps every
ghost by `path[htixxywyfp]`), rewind together on ACTION5, and once a ghost's path
is exhausted it STAYS put (holds its plate). Same `moves > Lg_i` seating fencepost
as L0, per ghost.

**Plan the machinery already supports:** bank ghost-A (record a walk onto plate
A → ACTION5), bank ghost-B (record a walk onto plate B → ACTION5), then a final
`kernels.configuration_path` over `(cell, moves)` with TWO barriers, each
`barrier_i` passable iff `moves > Lg_i`. The (cell, moves) formulation
generalises directly (both ghosts seat permanently, so both barriers stay open
after their own Lg) — NO `ghost_phase` dimension needed, exactly as L0.

**Why NOT built this pass — corrected: perception is CLEAN; the real blocker is
a NESTED two-circuit topology needing a general two-ghost planner + gated nested
discovery.** (Corrects an earlier over-claim in this pass that blamed
perception.) Live at L1, once the level-TRANSITION frame settles (the first
post-level-up frame is a giant-diff artifact that must be skipped — hold a
blocked move until the frame stabilises), perception is clean: exactly ONE
moving colour-9 player (column-8 corridor, moves one cell/step like L0) and ONE
static colour-9 goal at cell **(3,4)** (`gilbljmfbc`). There is NO non-player
mover — the "extra mover" seen initially was the transition frame. `nqtslsoqvv`/
`rinmohgkoo` do not move on L0-style probing. So the adapter needs only a
transition-frame skip in identify (minor).

The real work is the circuit. L1 topology (frame cells, 6px phase, OFF=4):
- Player starts col-8; reachable region over floor = **21 cells** (row-1
  corridor cols 1–8, the col-8 column, plus (2,6)(3,6)(4,7)(8,7)); the goal is
  NOT in it.
- The **two plates** (source `medyellngi` (13,37)/(37,25) → frame cells) are at
  **(4,6)** and **(6,2)**. Plate **(4,6) IS reachable** (floor neighbours (3,6)
  and (4,7) are in the region). Plate **(6,2) is NOT** — its floor neighbours

  (7,4),(8,1),(8,2),(8,3),(8,4),(8,5)}` that also holds the goal's approach
  cell **(3,3)** (goal (3,4) is entered from (3,3)).
- So the circuit is NESTED/SEQUENTIAL: seat ghost-B on the reachable plate (4,6)
  to open barrier-B (exposing the walled region + plate (6,2)); then — WHILE
  ghost-B holds barrier-B open during the ghost-A recording — walk through
  barrier-B to reach plate (6,2), seat ghost-A to open barrier-A (the goal
  approach); then the final player walk needs BOTH barriers, both ghosts seated.

The `(cell, moves)` planner with two barriers each gated on `moves > Lg_i`
covers the FINAL walk directly. The genuinely new build is (a) discovering a
plate that is only reachable AFTER another ghost is seated (gated discovery —

## Mechanics Hypothesis

Navigate the player through a wall maze (walls are a solid colour) to a hidden
GOAL cell (source: win when the player reaches the goal sprite offset by
(+1,+1)); the goal has no frame-distinguishable label. Because enemies react to
each move, the same player cell is safe or fatal depending on enemy phase — the
state is the joint (player, enemies), not the player cell alone. See
[[../game_types/sokoban]] and [[../concepts/frame_hashing]].
