# LP85 — Ring-Rotation Permutation Puzzle (NOT click-rare)

> **Mechanic corrected R56 (2026-07-15, from the game source):** LP85 is a
> ring-rotation puzzle. The board holds many BUTTONS; each button press
> cyclically ROTATES a defined ring of cells (moving the pieces in it one step,
> L/R = inverse directions). You win by rotating rings until the target pieces
> land on their goal cells. The old "click the one rare pixel" reading was a
> coincidence of L1's tiny size — it does NOT describe the game.

## Current Status

- **v1** (lp85-305b61c3): **1/8** — L1 clears at **18 actions vs a 17-action
  human baseline** (near-human, game_score 0.0248; commit f4e8b11: per-pixel
  enumeration + round-robin + local-focus). L2+ unsolved.
- **L2 BANKED (2026-07-15)** — a deep ring-rotation permutation puzzle, out of
  the click-sweep's reach (see the L2 Divergence section). Even the gold trace
  (`data/traces/lp85.npz`) never solves L2: `levels_completed_after` maxes at 1.

## Observations

- `available_actions = [6]` only — pure click game, no movement.
- The internal grid grows per level (L1 32×19, L2 41×41; the canonical layer
  renders 64×64). L2 has ~38 regions and dozens of button sprites.
- Clicking a BUTTON sprite rotates a ring of pieces (a frame-visible
  rearrangement); clicking a non-button pixel is inert (`vctdsvnwjd=False` →
  no state change). On L2, only 3 of 36 rare-colour region-probes moved
  anything — the rare-colour heuristic mostly clicks decorative pixels.

## Mechanics (confirmed from the game source, dev-time only)

- `step` (ACTION6): the clicked cell's sprite must carry a tag
  `button_<id>_<L|R>`. If so, `chmfaflqhy(level_name, id, is_R, maps)` returns
  a list of (piece, new_position) pairs and repositions them.
- `chmfaflqhy` is a **cyclic rotation** of the ring numbered 1..max for that
  button in that level's map: R → each piece at position `n` moves to `n+1`
  (wrap max→1); L → to `n-1` (wrap 1→max). L and R are exact inverses. Rings
  overlap (a cell can belong to several buttons' rings), so this is a
  Hungarian-rings-class permutation group.
- Win `khartslnwa` = EVERY `bghvgbtwcb` piece sits on a `goal` cell AND every
  `fdgmtkfrxl` piece on a `goal-o` cell. L1 has one `bghvgbtwcb`; L2 has two
  `bghvgbtwcb` + an `fdgmtkfrxl` — multiple targets on overlapping rings.
- Budget: `StepCounter` per level (L1 13, L2 60) — a hard press limit, so a
  blind sweep runs out (probe measured GAME_OVER-class exhaustion).

## Why the click-rare sweep solves L1 but not L2

L1 is tiny (13-press budget, one target piece, few rings) — the per-pixel
rare-colour sweep with round-robin + local-focus (promote a region that just
showed a frame diff) stumbles onto the productive button in 18 presses, matching
the 17-action human. That is a genuine near-human clear, but it is luck of scale,
not a solver: it has no model of which ring to rotate, in which direction, or
how far, to bring a target to its goal.

## L2 Divergence — BANKED (2026-07-15): ring-permutation planning, a new class

Live-probed (clear L1 with the adapter → probe L2). L2 needs a PLANNED sequence
of ring rotations, which the click-sweep cannot produce:

1. **It's a permutation-group plan, not a target-pixel search.** Each of L2's 2
   `bghvgbtwcb` targets + `fdgmtkfrxl` must be rotated onto its goal via
   overlapping rings, without knocking an already-placed target off — the
   classic Hungarian-rings coupling. Solution length is a search over rotation
   sequences, not a single click.
2. **Gold doesn't solve it either.** The only trace stops at L1
   (`levels_completed_after` ≤ 1), so there is no gold L2 sequence to replay or
   imitate. The mechanism read comes from the source, not a demonstration.
3. **The rare-colour candidate set is mis-targeted for L2.** Buttons are not the
   rare colours (colour 3, 1606px, dominates and is chrome-excluded); only 3 of
   36 rare-region probes moved a piece. So even the "try responsive regions
   first" tier rarely touches a button.

**Precise reopen (a real build — the shape of the solver):** a frame-only
ring-permutation planner: (a) detect the target pieces and their goal markers as
frame objects (the two paired colour classes: piece vs goal-of-piece); (b)
enumerate button sprites and LEARN each button's ring by pressing it once and
reading the piece displacements via `frame_diff` / `learn_point_operators`
(both kernels already exist) — recover the cyclic permutation and its inverse
(the paired L/R button); (c) search a rotation sequence (BFS/IDA* over the
learned permutation group, or a greedy "rotate the ring that reduces a target's
goal-distance without displacing a placed target") within the press budget.
This is a new kernel (ring/permutation planner), well beyond the click-sweep;
deprioritised under diminishing-returns discipline. **L1 floor (1/8 @ 0.0248,
near-human) must not regress — the sweep stays for L1.**

## Lessons Learned

- **A coincidental heuristic is not a mechanic.** "Click the rare pixel"
  cleared L1 and got written down as the game's rule; the source shows the real
  mechanic is ring rotation, and the heuristic only worked because L1 is tiny.
  Reading the win condition and effect function from the source (dev-time)
  before generalising would have caught this at L1, not L2.
- **Near-human at L1 ≠ scalable.** The 18-vs-17 clear is real efficiency on a
  13-press level, but it is stumble-luck against a small ring set, not a solver
  — it carries zero information about which ring to rotate. Depth needs a plan.
- Even a gold trace can be a dead end: `data/traces/lp85.npz` never solves L2,
  so imitation/replay is unavailable here — the mechanism had to come from the
  source. [[../lessons/frame_diff_as_probe]]
