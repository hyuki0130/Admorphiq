THE FINDING THE EARLIER PASS DID NOT HAVE
-----------------------------------------
With the CONVERGED model in hand at the OPENING board, level 4 costs EIGHT actions — half the
human 16. The plan is eight presses at k=1 and still eight at k=11, so the ten probe presses are
positionally NET-NEUTRAL. The entire 18 is an eight-press solution plus ten actions of model
identification. lp85 level 4 is not a planning problem at any point.

`total_if_final_model_here` = (k-1) + len(plan(board_k, final model)):
    k= 1 -> 8     k= 5 -> 12    k= 8 -> 15    k= 9 -> 15    k=10 -> 17    k=11 -> 18 (actual)
=> the cap needs the model plannable by propose 9, i.e. after EIGHT presses. Saving ONE press
   lands on 17 (0.8858); only saving TWO reaches 16.

THE CONFIRMATIONS ARE NOT DISCRETIONARY
---------------------------------------
`_next_probe` returns None the moment a plan exists, so every re-press happens BECAUSE no plan
exists. Measured: `plan_presses` under the tool's own model returns None at every propose until
press 10 is learned. This is a second, independent proof of the load-bearing finding — stronger
than "seven arms that cut them lost", because it shows they cannot be cut at all under the rule.

WHY THE TWO PRESSES CANNOT BE SAVED
-----------------------------------
The plan uses only (6,15) and (16,6). Controls (16,25) and (25,16) are pressed at proposes 3 and 4
and never used. The evidence that they are unnecessary — a plan — does not exist until propose 12.
The one rule that would skip them, a ready-check before a fresh press, is measured to cost level 1
(7 -> 21 actions, 0.9581) in the first pass.

TWO STRUCTURAL AXES CLOSED BY DIRECT MEASUREMENT
------------------------------------------------
1. INVERSE ADOPTION IS UNSOUND AT ONE PRESS (scripts/_lp85_inverse.py). The controls are rings x
   directions, so each permutation is its twin's exact inverse. Over all eight levels, 11 controls
   had some other control's recovered permutation invert into something that REPLAYS all of their
   own transitions — and 9 of the 11 disagree with the permutation the tool converges on, ALL FOUR
   of them on level 4. A single transition does not pin a permutation, and neither does its inverse.
2. THE RECOVERY COST CANNOT BE REWEIGHTED (scripts/_lp85_cost.py). The shipped least-total-distance
   rule recovers 14 of 34 controls EXACTLY from one press. Level 4's two USED controls are not among
   them, and their truth is not cheapest under any tried objective: it costs MORE total distance
   (76 vs 70) and has FEWER long steps (4 vs 7) than the permutation the rule picks. Charging per
   long step (2, 8, 64, 1000) recovers no more controls and breaks ones the shipped rule gets right,
   including level 1's and level 4's own two easy controls.
