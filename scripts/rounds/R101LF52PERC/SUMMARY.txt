Q1  WHAT DOES pegjump READ, AND WHY 2 OF 6? — IT IS THE CAMERA, AND NOTHING ELSE IS WRONG.
  The pipeline was instrumented stage by stage, because a pad can be lost in four different places
  that want four different repairs. At level-6 action 122:

      frame            14 socket squares,  2 discs
      on_phase_discs    2      off_phase_discs 0      disc_colour_refused 0
      board.pieces      2      model pieces    2      engine pads 6

  ⛔ NO FILTER DROPS ANYTHING. The briefing's candidate — a minimum-blob-size filter of the kind
  that makes lf52's four-two-pixel-blob move markers read as "there is no oracle" — is REFUTED for
  this tool: every disc the frame contains survives to the board, and the colour classifier refuses
  none of them.
  ⛔ AND NOTHING IS FORGOTTEN EITHER. `model_pieces_peak` is 2 across the WHOLE level (10 model
  reads, 12 board reads, 2 adopts, 1 install), so the model never held more and never threw any
  away. The board is 28 cells wide at pitch 6 against a 64-pixel screen and the camera sits at
  ox=-57; four of the six pads are off screen and were never on it while this tool held the board.

Q3  SO WHAT DOES SEPARATE IT? — SURVIVABILITY, AND ONLY ON A MAP KNOWN TO BE PARTIAL.
  scripts/_lf52_wsurv.py enumerated every distinct capture OUTCOME on the wide map within cost 26:
  there is exactly ONE, it is the fatal one, and `capture_reachable` after it is FALSE. So the
  guard that was measured INERT last round (refusals 0) is not wrong — it was asked about a plan
  that claimed to be a SOLUTION, where a guard on capture routes cannot see it.

  scripts/_lf52_partial.py then measured the signal live, because railpeg reaches this rule through
  a REFUTED claim and pegjump makes exactly ONE claim on this level — the losing move — so there is
  nothing to learn from. `runs_offscreen` (railpeg's `_offscreen` shape: track continuing past the
  window into cells the map has never held) is TRUE at all 10 of pegjump's level-6 decisions, and
  the counterfactual at the decision that mattered returns 1 candidate, survivable 0, all_fatal.

WHAT WAS BUILT, AND WHAT IT DID
  plan_moves gained `partial`: a solved state is no longer a solution (it stays worth playing
  toward), and the cheapest SURVIVABLE capture outcome is taken, returning None when none survives.

      captures 14/16 (railpeg) + 124 (pegjump)  ->  14/16 (railpeg) + 317 (GRAPH)
      lost_at 124 -> 317, restart 267 -> 339, score 0.272727 unchanged

  ⛔ pegjump stopped making the move and `graph` made the identical one 193 actions later. Then the
  reason the tool went idle was measured: eleven consecutive decisions with the known map fixed at
  26 cells. `explore_moves` maximises unknown territory next to a piece in MODEL coordinates, where
  no simulated move changes what is knowable — THE SIMULATION HAS NO CAMERA IN IT — so the tier
  meant to "go and look" cannot widen a window by construction.

  So railhead_moves was added: board a carrier, ride it toward the side where the TRACK leaves the
  screen. Two corrections, each measured:
    * reading the open end as "a CARRIER at an open end" (railpeg's form) made the tier silent —
      lf52's carriers sit mid-strip while the rails run off both sides. The open end belongs to the
      track; the carrier rides to it.
    * filtering the ride through `_drive` made it silent again — the drive model rolls a carrier
      only onto cells the map already calls track, so every ride is one cell short of the only
      place worth going. The ride is emitted UNSIMULATED, bounded by the barren counter.
